# Footers and Instant Contact: Research for 24/7 Car Rental, Tirana

Researched 2026-08-05. Live footers were fetched and read, not recalled. Sites that returned
403 to automated fetching (sixt.com, sixt.co.uk, hertz.com, rentfromlocals.al, apexcar.gr) are
noted where their absence matters. Everything below is sourced at the bottom.

Client facts assumed: 19 cars, two locations (Tirana International Airport; Rruga Njazi Meka,
Tiranë), open 24 hours, phone +355 68 500 0700, email info@24-7rentalcar.com, Instagram
@24_7carrental and @247.car.rental, bookings taken by phone and WhatsApp, no online payment.

### 1.2 The pattern that separates the two groups

**Big brands treat the footer as a sitemap and an SEO surface.** Avis ships 40 location links;
Rentalcars ships 100 across five clusters. That works because each link is a page with real
inventory behind it. It is also where the compliance junk lives (accessibility statement,
modern slavery statement, "Your Privacy Choices", fraud warnings, investor and careers links).
None of that is transferable to a 19-car operator, and copying its *shape* is the single
clearest tell that a small site was built from a template.

**Small operators treat the footer as the contact card.** In every regional operator examined,
the largest and most useful footer block is the address + hours + phone block, and the good ones
attach a Google Maps link per location (tirental.al, enjoyridecorfu.com, ivrentacar.com). Two
sites put a named human next to the number (ivrentacar.com). That is the strongest small-operator
move in the whole sample: it converts "a website" into "a person who will answer".

**The pre-booking anxiety block is the one small operators mostly miss.** Only
enjoyridecorfu.com groups it properly ("Useful Information: FAQs, Insurance, Extras, Where We
Deliver, Terms"), and only cria.al surfaces the two things renters actually search for in
Albania as footer links ("No Credit Card", "Low/No Deposit"). Everyone else buries insurance
and deposit inside a wall-of-text Terms page or omits it.

### 1.3 The trust gap, and why it is worth exploiting

Of the six Albanian and Montenegrin operators inspected, **zero publish a company registration
number, VAT/NIPT, or the name of their insurer in the footer.** Not one.

This matters because of how the market works:
- NIPT (the Albanian tax ID issued at QKB registration) is mandatory on every fiscal invoice and
  on the e-invoicing / fiskalizim chain, so the company certainly has one. It is also publicly
  verifiable through free NIPT lookup tools. Printing it is free and checkable.
- Albanian car rental is a market with a documented trust problem: independent guides warn that
  "the worst problems are not outright scams, they are weak policies disguised by a polished
  website", and specifically flag deposit holds of 250-1500 EUR and CDW excess of 500-1500 EUR
  as the thing renters get surprised by. Full/Super CDW runs 10-20 EUR/day and still commonly
  excludes tyres, glass and underbody.
- TPL is legally required and always included in Albania. Saying so plainly costs nothing and
  pre-empts the most common question.

So: legal entity + NIPT + registered address + the actual CDW excess number + insurer name is a
trust package that no direct competitor currently offers. It is the cheapest differentiator
available on this project.

### 1.4 What renters actually want at the bottom of the page

Synthesis of the trust-and-transparency research: users verify legitimacy first, compare price
second, and check whether the terms fit their trip third. The concrete items they hunt for are
taxes, insurance options, mileage rules, fuel policy, deposit amount, driver age minimum,
airport surcharges, and the cancellation cut-off. The footer's job is not to hold all of that.
Its job is to make every one of those items one click away, and to prove a human answers.

**Genuinely useful in a small-operator footer:** addresses with map links, hours, phone, WhatsApp,
email, insurance/deposit/cancellation links, driver requirements, airport pickup instructions,
legal entity + registration number, payment methods actually accepted, a linked review score.

**Filler in a small-operator footer:** newsletter signup, app badges, careers, affiliate program,
press, investor relations, "our values", duplicated navigation, unlinked award graphics, and
location link clusters for cities where there is no office and no page.

### 2.2 Evidence on whether floating buttons help or annoy

Baymard's large-scale usability testing of live chat found three implementations that damage the
experience, in descending order of harm:

1. **Overlay dialogs.** Test subject: *"It's like they're pushing you when you could just be
   browsing… Feel like they're forcing you."*
2. **Dialog pop-ups.** Test subject: *"Oh, god, I hate these, go away. NOO! Go. Away. If I wanted
   chat I would ask for it."* Less confident users never dismissed them and browsed with reduced
   screen space for the rest of the session.
3. **Sticky/floating elements.** Less interruptive, but they were observed obstructing
   autocomplete suggestions, colour swatches and filter buttons, and the damage concentrates on
   mobile where space is already scarce. One subject: *"I can't read it. Can I move that? No."*

Baymard's recommendation is unambiguous: **live chat performs best user-initiated, not
site-initiated**, and the placements that work are footers, headers and help sections, where the
user is already looking for help. If site-initiated chat is used, it should be excluded from
checkout and filtering pages, shown once, and delayed.

Separately, bottom-right is the learned convention and sits outside the F-shaped scan path, which
is why it is the low-damage position when a floating button is used at all.

**The conversion numbers going around are vendor marketing, not research.** "98% open rate vs
21% email", "40% conversion uplift", "click rates 5-15%, conversion 3-7%" all trace to chat-tool
vendors (Chatarmin, Wapikit, Zoho, various widget sellers) with no methodology published. Do not
put these in a client proposal as facts. The defensible argument is behavioural, not statistical:
this business already takes its bookings on WhatsApp, so the site's job is to hand the customer
to the channel the business actually operates on, with the context pre-filled.

### 2.3 wa.me mechanics and prefill practice

- Format: `https://wa.me/<number>?text=<url-encoded message>`. Number in full international form,
  **no plus sign, no spaces, no dashes**. For this client: `https://wa.me/355685000700`.
- The prefilled text is a **suggestion**. It lands in the user's compose box and they can edit or
  delete it before sending. So it must read as something a customer would plausibly write, in
  first person, not as a marketing string.
- Keep it short and specific. The prefill's value is that it saves the *operator* a round trip:
  it should carry the car, the page URL, and slots for dates.
- Encode properly (`%20`, `%0A` for line breaks). Test on both iOS and Android.
- **Degradation is real and must be planned for.** On mobile the WhatsApp app must be installed
  or the link dead-ends. On desktop it opens WhatsApp Web, which requires the user to be logged
  in. Therefore WhatsApp must never be the only contact path on any screen; a `tel:` link and the
  email address always sit adjacent.

### 2.4 Accessibility requirements for a floating button

- **WCAG 2.2 SC 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum), Level AA.** A sticky or floating element
  must not completely hide the component that has keyboard focus. This is the specific criterion
  a floating chat bubble most often fails, and it fails hardest over form fields, which is
  exactly where this site's booking enquiry lives.
- **SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum), Level AA** requires 24x24 CSS px; 44x44 is the mobile
  guidance figure and the right target here. Padding counts toward the target.
- It must be a real `<a href>` with a descriptive `aria-label`
  (e.g. `aria-label="Chat with 24/7 Car Rental on WhatsApp"`), not an icon-only `<div>` with a
  click handler. Icon-only with no accessible name is the most common failure.
- It must not overlap a mobile sticky booking bar. Pick one persistent bottom-of-viewport
  element per screen, never two.
- It needs a visible focus ring and must be reachable in a sensible tab order, not tab-trapped.
- Third-party accessibility/chat overlay widgets do not confer compliance and carry their own
  legal-risk literature. Build the button; do not install a widget.

## PART 3 — RECOMMENDATION

### (a) Footer structure for 24/7 Car Rental, in order

**Block 1 — Reach a person now**
Full-width top row, visually the heaviest thing in the footer, because this business converts on
contact and not on a cart.
- `+355 68 500 0700` as a large `tel:` link, formatted for reading.
- WhatsApp button on the same number (pending confirmation, see open questions).
- `info@24-7rentalcar.com`.
- One line of genuinely new information beneath: that the phone is answered at any hour, including
  after the last flight lands. That is a fact and a differentiator, not a caption.

*Why it earns its place:* every regional competitor buries contact in a nav column or the header.
This business's entire operating model is "call us, we answer". Lead with it.

**Block 2 — Where to find us**
Two locations, each as a small card with full address, one line on what happens there, opening
hours (24h, both), and a Google Maps link. Copy the tirental.al / enjoyridecorfu.com pattern of a
map link per location, which is the one thing local operators do better than the globals.
- Tirana International Airport (TIA), Rinas.
- Rruga Njazi Meka, Tiranë.

**Block 3 — Before you book**
The anxiety block. Links only, but each to a real page with real numbers:
What's included in the price · Insurance and excess · Security deposit · Driver requirements
(age, licence held for how long, documents) · Fuel and mileage policy · Cross-border travel
(Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Greece) · Cancellation · Airport pickup and delivery · FAQ.

*Why it earns its place:* this is precisely the list renters hunt for, and it is the block
cria.al monetises with "No Credit Card" and "Low/No Deposit" as footer links. Given this company
takes no online payment, "No online payment, no card charged in advance" belongs here as a
headline link, not an apology.

**Block 4 — Cars and roads**
The honest SEO cluster. Two short lists:
- Car classes that actually exist in the 19-car fleet (economy, automatic, SUV, 7-seater, or
  whatever the real breakdown is).
- Real route and destination pages, only ones that will be built: Tirana to Sarandë and Ksamil,
  Tirana to Theth, the Llogara Pass and SH8 coast road, Berat, Gjirokastër, Tirana to Ohrid /
  Prizren / Kotor.

*Why it earns its place:* it is the small-operator equivalent of Avis's 40 city links, but every
link resolves to a page that answers a genuine pre-booking question ("which of your cars can do
the Theth road"). It also connects the roads page to the fleet, which is the site's main internal
linking job. **Only ship the links whose pages exist.** A cluster of dead or thin pages is the
same category of filler as the departure board.

**Block 5 — The company**
The trust block that no competitor in this market currently has.
- Registered legal entity name, NIPT, registered address.
- Insurance line: TPL included by law; CDW status and the actual excess figure in euros; insurer
  name if the client will give it.
- Payment accepted at pickup: cash (EUR / ALL) and cards, stated explicitly.
- Linked Google review score with its real count, only if it exists. Link the actual Google
  Business Profile, never a static star graphic.
- One Instagram handle plus TikTok if active.

**Block 6 — Legal bar**
One line: © 2026 [entity name] · NIPT [number] · Terms and Conditions · Privacy Policy · Cookie
notice (only if analytics or pixels are actually running).

#### Open questions for the client (do not invent these)

1. Registered legal entity name and NIPT.
2. Full street address for the city office, including building number and postcode, plus the
   exact airport pickup point: is there a desk in arrivals, a kerbside meet, or a shuttle to a
   nearby lot? Is there an after-hours or out-of-hours airport delivery fee?
3. Is +355 68 500 0700 the WhatsApp number, and is it a WhatsApp Business account?
4. Security deposit amount and how it is taken (cash, card hold, or none).
5. CDW excess amount, what is excluded (tyres, glass, underbody are the usual carve-outs),
   whether a zero-excess upgrade is offered and at what daily price.
6. Insurer name, and whether they issue a green card for cross-border travel, to which countries,
   at what fee.
7. Minimum driver age, minimum licence held, and whether an IDP is required for non-EU licences.
8. Payment methods genuinely accepted at pickup, and which currencies.
9. Fuel policy and mileage policy.
10. Cancellation terms and cut-off.
11. Which Instagram handle is canonical (@24_7carrental or @247.car.rental). Two live accounts
    split the follower signal and confuse the footer; recommend consolidating.
12. Whether a Google Business Profile exists with reviews, and its URL.
13. Real fleet breakdown by class and transmission, so Block 4 lists true categories.

### (b) Contact placement plan, page by page

**Global rules.**
- Header carries the phone number as visible text with a `tel:` link on desktop; a 44px phone
  icon button on mobile.
- Exactly one persistent bottom-of-viewport element per screen. Never a floating WhatsApp bubble
  and a sticky booking bar at the same time.
- Floating button, when present: bottom-right, appears only after the hero scrolls out, 48px, a
  real `<a>` with `aria-label`, no pulse animation, no fake unread badge, no auto-opening
  greeting bubble, no third-party widget.
- WhatsApp never appears without an adjacent `tel:` link, because wa.me dead-ends without the app
  or a WhatsApp Web session.
- The footer contact row appears on every page. That is Baymard's endorsed user-initiated
  placement and it does the work the floating button gets credit for.

**Home.** Header phone. Hero primary CTA is "See the cars"; WhatsApp is the secondary. Floating
button after the hero. Footer Block 1.
Prefill: `Hello, I'd like to rent a car in Tirana. Pick-up date: / Drop-off date: / Pick-up:
airport or city office`

**Fleet list.** Header phone. **No WhatsApp button per card** (19 green buttons is visual noise
and it is the pattern that reads as a template). One WhatsApp CTA at the end of the list, framed
as help rather than sales. Floating button on.
Prefill: `Hello, I'm looking at your fleet and I'm not sure which car fits. We are (number of
people) with (luggage) and driving to (where).`

**Individual car page.** This is where the per-product button earns its place, and no competitor
in the sample does it. Two equal-weight buttons, WhatsApp and Call, directly under the price.
On mobile they become the sticky bottom bar once the price scrolls past; the floating bubble is
suppressed on this page to avoid two persistent elements.
Prefill must carry the car and the URL, since that is what removes a round trip for the operator:
`Hello, I'd like to book the [Make Model Year, transmission]. Pick-up: / Drop-off: / Location:
airport or city office. [full URL of the car page]`

**Booking / enquiry flow.** Floating button **off** (Baymard: exclude checkout and filtering
pages). Under the submit button, one line offering the same thing by message. The high-value move
is on the confirmation screen: a WhatsApp button prefilled with what the user just typed, so the
enquiry survives even if the email never lands. This is the single best use of prefill on the site.
Prefill (confirmation screen): `Hello, I just sent a booking request on your website. Name: [x].
Car: [y]. Pick-up: [date, location]. Drop-off: [date, location].`

**Roads / destinations page.** Long-form reading, so no sticky bottom bar. Floating button on.
Inline contextual CTA at the end of each route section, tied to the road.
Prefill: `Hello, I'm planning to drive [Tirana to Theth]. Which of your cars handles that road?`

**About page.** Keep the floating button for consistency but the page's real close is a human:
the two-location block, the number, and a name to ask for. A named person outperforms a widget
here, and ivrentacar.com is the proof from this same market.
Prefill: `Hello, I have a question before I book.`

**404.** Contact is the entire content of the page: phone, WhatsApp, a link back to the fleet.
No floating button, it would duplicate the page body.
Prefill: `Hello, I hit a broken link on your website while looking for [ ]. Can you help?`

**Operational note.** The number on the site must be the same one running WhatsApp Business, with
business hours, a greeting message, and an away message configured. A WhatsApp link to a number
that nobody watches is worse than no link.

### (c) Footer patterns to avoid, because they read as filler or AI-generated

1. **The fake departure board** ("19 CARS / 18 AUTOMATIC / 3 ROUTES"). Data-theater: numbers
   nobody asked for, that go stale the day a car is sold, presented with airport-flap styling that
   implies a scale the business does not have. The client is right. Also on this list: animated
   count-up stat rows and any unsourced metric.
2. **Invented location clusters.** Footer links to "Car rental in Durrës / Vlorë / Sarandë" when
   there is no office and no page. Copying Avis's 40-link geography onto a two-location business.
3. **Newsletter signup.** A 19-car rental firm has nothing to send weekly.
4. **App store badges.** There is no app.
5. **Payment logos for methods not accepted.** Apple Pay / Google Pay / Amex icons on a business
   that takes cash and card at the desk.
6. **Self-issued trust badges.** "100% Secure", decorative SSL padlocks, a made-up "Best in
   Albania 2026" award, star ratings not linked to a real review profile.
7. **"Trusted by" logo strips** and partner logos the business has no relationship with.
8. **The four-channel icon tower** floating on the left edge (WhatsApp + Viber + Telegram +
   Messenger + Facebook). Viber is legitimate in this market; five stacked bubbles are not.
9. **The pulsing green bubble** with a fake "1" unread badge and an auto-opening "Hi 👋 How can I
   help?" after three seconds. Baymard's test subjects reacted to exactly this with hostility.
10. **Dead or duplicated links.** "Contact / Contact Us / Get in touch" three times in one footer;
    links to pages that were never built.
11. **Corporate blocks copied from Hertz.** Careers, Investors, Press, Affiliate Program,
    Modern Slavery Statement, Do Not Sell My Information. None apply.
12. **The whole navigation dumped twice** as a pseudo-sitemap.
13. **Subtitles under footer headings.** "Locations" followed by "where you can find us".
    Say it once in the heading.
14. **Em dashes** anywhere in the copy.
15. **"Book your dream car in minutes" / "24/7 excellence" / "seamless"** style filler copy in
    the footer strapline. If the footer has a line of copy, it should carry a fact.
